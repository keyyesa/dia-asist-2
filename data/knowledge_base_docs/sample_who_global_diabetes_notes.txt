Global Burden
Diabetes prevalence has risen substantially worldwide over recent decades, driven
by population aging, urbanization, and increases in overweight and obesity. Type 2
diabetes accounts for the large majority of diabetes cases globally, while type 1
diabetes results from autoimmune destruction of insulin-producing cells and
typically requires lifelong insulin therapy.

Prevention
Population-level prevention strategies for type 2 diabetes commonly emphasize
promoting physical activity, healthy diet, and maintaining a healthy body weight,
along with policies that support access to affordable nutritious food and safe
spaces for physical activity. Early identification of prediabetes allows for
lifestyle intervention that can reduce or delay progression to type 2 diabetes.

Access to Care
Equitable access to affordable diagnostics (such as blood glucose and HbA1c
testing), essential medicines (including insulin and oral glucose-lowering
agents), and trained healthcare workers is considered essential to reducing the
global burden of diabetes-related complications and mortality, particularly in
low- and middle-income settings.

Patient Education
Patient education on self-management -- including recognizing signs of
hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia, proper medication use, foot care, and the
importance of regular follow-up -- is emphasized as a key component of reducing
diabetes-related complications at the population level.
